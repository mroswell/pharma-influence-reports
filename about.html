Sources, Method & Attribution

What this is, where the evidence comes from, and how to read it.

The compilationWhat this is

This site collects five connected, evidence-based reports prepared in 2026. Four are structured reports; the fifth is a curated set of follow-up questions and answers. They share a single method: rely on primary data and peer-reviewed literature, present both what the evidence shows and where it runs out, and keep uncertainty intact rather than resolving it for effect.

Important notice

All material here is for informational purposes only. It does not constitute medical, legal, or political advice. Nothing on this site recommends for or against any vaccine, candidate, or party. Readers should consult a qualified professional before making decisions about vaccination, treatment, or anything else discussed here.

MethodHow the reports were built

The campaign-finance reports rely on Federal Election Commission filings as compiled by OpenSecrets, plus official U.S. Senate roll-call records. The Canadian report relies on Elections Canada disclosures, the Registry of Lobbyists, and Canadian statute. The COVID-19 report draws exclusively on peer-reviewed medical literature, official regulatory documents, court records, and investigative reporting in major medical journals — not on social-media claims, political statements, or unverified sources.

Two caveats run through everything here. First, correlation is not causation: patterns of giving and voting do not, by themselves, prove that any contribution caused any vote. Second, absence of evidence is not evidence of absence: where the surveillance record is inadequate, the honest position is that a question cannot be answered — not that it has been answered either way.
