Pharmaceutical Industry Campaign Contributions to U.S. Senators

The 2023–2024 election cycle, senator by senator.

Based on FEC data compiled by OpenSecrets.org Report prepared: August 2026

Approximately 94 out of 100 current U.S. senators received some level of campaign contribution from the pharmaceutical industry during the 2023–2024 election cycle. These contributions arrive through company Political Action Committees (PACs), employee donations, and affiliated individuals giving over $200 — because direct corporate donations to candidates are prohibited under federal law.

ContextImportant notes

Both parties are heavily represented. Democrats and Republicans appear in roughly equal numbers across the top recipients, though Democrats slightly dominated the top positions due to competitive Senate races on the ballot in 2024.

The top three no longer serve. Bob Casey, Jon Tester, and Sherrod Brown all lost their 2024 re-election bids. The largest recipient among currently serving senators is therefore Bill Cassidy (R-Louisiana) at $329,842.

The total pharmaceutical and health-product spend across all federal candidates in 2023–24 was about $86.5 million — roughly $16 million of it routed directly to candidates through PACs.

Corporations cannot give directly. Under federal law, all contributions counted here flow through company PACs or from employees and affiliated individuals associated with pharmaceutical companies.

Almost no one is untouched. Only a small handful of senators — estimated at fewer than six — appear to have received no identifiable pharmaceutical-industry contributions during this cycle.

Note on data currency

Contribution totals reflect filings processed through the end of the 2023–2024 election cycle. Late-filed amendments may marginally alter individual totals. All figures are reported in U.S. dollars.

MethodAbout the data

This report is based on Federal Election Commission (FEC) filing data as compiled and analysed by OpenSecrets, a non-partisan, non-profit research organisation tracking money in U.S. politics. Figures cover the full 2023–2024 election cycle and include contributions from pharmaceutical and health-product company PACs, as well as donations of $200 or more from employees and individuals identified as affiliated with the pharmaceutical industry.

Prepared for informational purposes only. Figures may be revised as late filings are processed. Not intended as legal or political advice.
